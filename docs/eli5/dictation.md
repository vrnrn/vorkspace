# Dictation

Turn on **Dictation** in **Settings → General**. It uses speech recognition
provided by macOS 26 or newer. Your Mac must support that engine and your system
language. Speech stays on your Mac.

Click a text field, press **Control Shift Space**, and speak when the notch's
microphone turns red. Words appear as you speak. Press the shortcut again, or
click the microphone, to finish. Escape or the cross cancels. Typing, clicking
elsewhere or switching apps also stops it. Words already entered remain.
You can change the shortcut in Keybindings.

Settings helps you allow Microphone and Accessibility for **vorkspace Clipboard**.
If your language needs speech files, choose **Download language**. macOS handles
the files and keeps them up to date.

For names Apple spells incorrectly, add custom words and save them:

```text
Vorkspace
work space → Vorkspace
clawed → Claude
```

The first line fixes capitalization. The next two replace a specific misheard
phrase with your preferred spelling. These corrections work on the words Apple
recognizes; they do not teach its speech model a new sound. Changes take effect
the next time you start dictating.

A small helper keeps dictation available after Vorkspace quits, even with
clipboard history off. To stop it, disable both features or use Quit in the
clipboard menu. It never keeps the microphone open while waiting for you.
Vox and dictation take turns using the notch and microphone.

Most text fields accept the result directly. Apps with their own input systems
need checking in practice. Dictation stops when you take over the field and does
not retry uncertain input.
